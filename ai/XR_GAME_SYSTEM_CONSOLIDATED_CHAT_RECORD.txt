CORE GAME / WORLD DIRECTION
---------------------------
The broader game is a persistent XR world/system where players can:
- create vehicles;
- create environments;
- explore;
- gather resources;
- encounter creatures;
- build and use workshops;
- maintain persistent systems;
- create and manipulate structures;
- save, load, rebuild, and summon creations.

The vehicle-builder is a foundational system, not a secondary creator mode.

The desired player loop is:
1. Enter a build area.
2. Open a real player-facing catalog.
3. Select a construction piece.
4. Place it.
5. Rotate and align it.
6. Snap it deterministically.
7. Continue assembling.
8. Add functional parts such as wheels, helm/control station, engine, propeller or other propulsion.
9. Save the result as a blueprint.
10. Load/edit/rebuild the blueprint.
11. Summon the completed vehicle into the world.
12. Operate the physical vehicle.

CONSTRUCTION SYSTEM
-------------------
There are three intentional construction scales:
- 0.001 m³
- 0.1 m³
- 1 m³

These are named/discrete construction scales exactly as defined by the Product Owner. They must not be silently reinterpreted as ordinary cube-volume measurements.

A critical architectural distinction:
- construction envelope = spatial contract;
- actual mesh/geometry = geometry contained inside the envelope.

A block may contain arbitrary geometry inside its envelope, including actual automobile body-surface geometry such as:
- hood;
- fender;
- body panels;
- curved exterior surfaces.

A whole vehicle body should be breakable into construction pieces and reassembled in different configurations.

BLOCK SHAPES
------------
Each scale should eventually support a broad catalog of standardized forms inspired by the breadth of construction-piece catalogs, without requiring copied assets.

Required/desired families include:
- slopes;
- convex pieces;
- concave pieces;
- sphere/rounded forms;
- diamond forms;
- prism / eight-sided prism-like forms;
- "chestahedron" (retain this user-defined term rather than silently replacing it);
- rectangles;
- half pieces;
- quarter pieces;
- elbows/connectors;
- vehicle-specific body pieces.

The system must allow half and quarter pieces to stack and align predictably with larger and smaller pieces.

OCCUPANCY / TOPOLOGY
--------------------
Occupancy is explicit and deterministic. A shape may occupy only selected regions inside its construction envelope.

Conceptual example:
  [occupied][occupied][occupied]
  [occupied]          [occupied]

The block definition should support:
- occupied_cells;
- construction scale;
- envelope dimensions;
- attachment faces/points;
- allowed rotations;
- collision representation;
- compatibility rules;
- physics metadata;
- mesh/geometry reference.

A construction envelope can also serve as a blueprint-scale authoring space in which terrain, meshes, textures, and other geometry are authored.

PLAYER-FACING CATALOG
---------------------
A construction system without a menu/catalog is not useful to the player.

The catalog should expose:
- size selector: [0.001] [0.1] [1.0];
- categories such as basic, angular, curved, connectors, vehicle;
- shape thumbnails;
- 3D preview where practical;
- place/add action;
- player-created pieces.

The CREATE BLOCK flow should support:
1. choose envelope/scale;
2. author or modify geometry;
3. define occupied cells;
4. define attachment faces;
5. preview;
6. test;
7. save to catalog.

MY BLOCKS should expose player-created/custom pieces.

Built-in procedural shapes should be generated first so the system is not blocked by external asset hunting.

BLUEPRINT SYSTEM
----------------
Blueprints must preserve construction intent, not merely a flattened final mesh.

Conceptual blueprint fields:
- blueprint ID/name;
- block/part instances;
- block definition IDs;
- construction scale;
- grid/envelope position;
- rotation/orientation;
- occupied-cell pattern;
- attachment relationships;
- custom geometry references;
- material references;
- functional component metadata;
- physics metadata;
- version/schema.

This permits:
- rebuilding;
- editing;
- validation;
- migration;
- deterministic reconstruction;
- AI inspection and manipulation.

VEHICLE PHYSICS
---------------
Assembled vehicles should become physical objects rather than decorative meshes.

Aggregate vehicle physics should account for:
- component/block mass;
- center of mass;
- collision representation;
- inertia;
- angular behavior;
- wheels;
- suspension;
- tire/contact friction;
- motor/engine force;
- steering;
- angular velocity;
- propellers;
- other propulsion mechanisms;
- helm/control station.

Block definitions may carry:
- mass;
- material;
- collision;
- physics metadata.

The assembled vehicle derives aggregate physical properties from its components.

VR PLAYER / AVATAR FOUNDATION
-----------------------------
The avatar is a physical gameplay system, not merely a visual model.

The foundation needs:
- HMD/head tracking;
- torso/body representation;
- head/body collision;
- mass;
- gravity;
- velocity;
- ground detection;
- friction/contact;
- physical hands;
- controller targets;
- stable gameplay inertia.

Two systems must remain distinct:

1. Tracking / IK skeleton
   - visual/anatomical pose;
   - head, torso, shoulders, arms, elbows, forearms, hands;
   - future hips, knees, feet, full-body IK.

2. Physical player body
   - collision;
   - mass;
   - gravity;
   - velocity;
   - physical interaction.

Scientific human density is not required. Consistent gameplay mass and inertia are required.

SHOULDER / ELBOW / CONTROLLER IK
---------------------------------
The arm system identified in discussion with Ash and Oleg is a core avatar-foundation system.

Desired chain:
HMD
 -> Head
 -> Neck
 -> Chest/Torso
 -> Shoulder
 -> Upper Arm
 -> Elbow
 -> Forearm
 -> Hand
 -> Controller

The controller provides a hand target. It must not simply replace or rigidly parent the entire arm.

Shoulders should be estimated from:
- headset position;
- torso position;
- avatar proportions;
- calibration.

Elbow solving requires:
- elbow pole / preferred bend direction;
- upper-arm length;
- forearm length;
- joint limits;
- shoulder constraints;
- collision/avoidance;
- smoothing;
- stability against sudden elbow flips.

Required result:
- shoulders appear anatomically mounted;
- elbows bend in a controlled direction;
- elbows do not collapse into the torso;
- arms do not visibly clip through the avatar;
- hands track controller targets;
- arms move naturally around the character.

The arm system is important for:
- grabbing vehicle parts;
- pulling/climbing;
- pushing;
- reaching;
- entering vehicles;
- sitting;
- operating controls;
- leaning around obstacles;
- physical environmental interaction.

FINAL DESIGN INTENT
-------------------
The central idea is a Quest-first XR system in which the player is physically present in a persistent world and can physically interact with the world, their avatar, vehicles, construction pieces, tools, inventory, and authored creations.

The construction system is not simply a collection of decorative cubes. Each piece has a spatial envelope, explicit occupancy, attachment logic, geometry, and physical metadata. A vehicle is not merely a mesh: it is a structured assembly whose blueprint can be saved, rebuilt, edited, validated, and physically simulated.

The avatar is not merely a camera and floating hands. Tracking, IK, physical collision, mass, gravity, and hand interaction form a unified foundation. Shoulder placement and elbow solving are first-class systems so the arms remain visually and physically coherent while following controller targets.

The development system is not merely an AI that writes code. It is intended to become a controlled engineering pipeline with project memory, explicit state, scoped specialist agents, verification, evidence, GitHub source-of-truth, and eventually autonomous continuation when tasks are authorized and machine-verifiable.

The most important rule throughout the project is simple:
BUILD SMALL -> VERIFY FOR REAL -> RECORD EVIDENCE -> THEN EXPAND.
